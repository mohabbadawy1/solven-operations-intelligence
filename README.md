# Solven Real Estate Intelligence

An enterprise-grade operations intelligence platform for multi-project
real estate developers, built by **Solven**, an AI automation company,
as a working demonstration for a prospective real estate client. It
turns a developer's raw commercial, collections, construction, and
customer-experience data into a board-ready **Real Estate Executive
Intelligence Report** (JSON, Markdown, HTML, and a print-quality PDF),
with every number in the report traceable to a deterministic Python
calculation and every sentence of narrative written by an LLM that is
structurally prevented from inventing or recalculating a figure.

All company, project, customer, broker, and financial data in this
repository is **entirely synthetic**, generated by
`generate_real_estate_data.py`. No real company or real financial
figures are represented anywhere.

## The Scenario

The demo models **Horizon Developments**, a fictional Egyptian/MENA
real-estate developer running five active projects across residential,
seasonal-coastal, and commercial segments:

| Project | Segment | Location | Story |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Aurelia New Cairo (AUR)** | Premium residential | New Cairo | Strong demand, but first-response times are degrading and premium leads sit unworked behind an overloaded sales team — a capacity problem, not a demand problem. |
| **Coastline North (CST)** | Seasonal coastal | North Coast | Summer-driven demand (a ~3x lead-volume swing across the year) with cancellations and delinquency rising in the months after each peak season. |
| **Vertex Business District (VTX)** | Commercial/administrative | New Administrative Capital | Broker-dependent, with one broker sourcing a disproportionate share of reservations at an elevated cancellation rate and discount level. |
| **Haven West (HVW)** | Mid-market residential | 6th of October City | High lead volume, but one sales team is closing reservations with low down payments, heavy discounting, and long payment plans — driving cancellations that peak 45–90 days after reservation and rising receivables delinquency. |
| **Meridian Residences (MER)** | Mature residential, near handover | New Cairo | Two specific buildings are running 30+ days behind their construction schedule, exposing contracted (and partly already-collected) value to a delayed handover. |

These five problems are **engineered into the synthetic data**, not
hard-coded into the analytics or the report. The correlation engine
(`analysis/correlations.py`) discovers all five independently, using
detectors that trigger on statistical patterns (composite z-scores,
correlation strength, concentration share, coefficient of variation)
evaluated against *every* project/broker/building in the data — none
of the detection logic references a specific entity name.

## Architecture

```
        data/*.csv (14 relational datasets)
                    │  pandas.read_csv
                    ▼
   analysis/*.py — nine domain analytics engines
   (sales, funnel, inventory, marketing, sales team/
   broker, collections, cancellations, construction/
   handover, customer experience) + data_quality.py
                    │  structured KPIs, rankings, health scores
                    ▼
   analysis/executive_scoring.py — materiality-weighted
   Overall Business Health across the nine domains
                    │
                    ▼
   analysis/correlations.py — cross-domain root-cause,
   financial-exposure, and recommendation engine
                    │  fully-computed report skeleton (numbers only)
                    ▼
   ai/report_generator.py — curates that skeleton into
   an AI context, calls Groq for narrative text ONLY,
   validates and merges the response back in
                    │
                    ▼
   ai/html_report_renderer.py + ai/pdf_report_renderer.py
   — deterministic rendering to HTML and a print-quality
   A4 portrait PDF
                    │
                    ▼
   outputs/executive_report.{json,md,html,pdf}
```

Each layer depends only on the layer below it. **Python calculates.
The AI writes.** This split is structural, not a prompting convention:

- `analysis/*.py` computes every KPI, health score, ranking, root
  cause, confidence score, and financial-exposure figure that appears
  in the final report, entirely in Python (pandas/numpy), before the
  AI is ever called.
- `ai/report_generator.py` builds a "report skeleton" from those
  already-computed numbers, then asks the LLM for narrative text only
  (headline, executive summary, department briefings, root-cause and
  recommendation framing) — the model is never given a raw dataset and
  is never asked to calculate anything. Its JSON response is validated
  and matched back onto the skeleton **by title**, so it cannot alter,
  invent, or misattribute a number, ranking, or entity name; it can
  only add narrative prose.
- A best-effort hallucination check (`_find_unverified_numbers`)
  additionally scans the AI's narrative text for any number that
  doesn't trace back to the curated context, and discloses any
  findings in the report's own Appendix rather than silently trusting
  the model.
- n8n (not included in this repository) owns **orchestration only**:
  scheduling, triggering `POST /run-analysis`, downloading the
  generated PDF artifact, emailing it, and handling retries/failure
  notifications. It performs no analytics and calls no LLM directly.

## Datasets

`generate_real_estate_data.py` produces 14 relational CSVs in `data/`,
spanning 18–24 months of transactional history with real monthly/
seasonal patterns:

`projects`, `units`, `customers`, `brokers`, `sales_agents`,
`campaigns`, `leads`, `sales`, `payment_schedules`, `collections`,
`construction_milestones`, `customer_cases`, `handovers`, `snagging`.

Every field is derived causally (a lead's response time depends on its
project and how loaded that project's sales team is; a sale's discount
and down payment depend on which sales team or broker closed it; an
installment's overdue probability depends on the buyer's down payment,
plan length, and discount) rather than sampled independently, so the
five scenarios above are genuinely discoverable by descriptive
statistics — not injected as a label.

## Analytics Modules

```
analysis/
    _shared.py                        # domain-agnostic math/formatting primitives
    _config.py                        # loads config/real_estate_demo.yml once
    sales_performance.py              # commercial KPIs, pricing/discount analytics, target attainment
    lead_funnel.py                    # funnel conversion, response SLA, agent workload/capacity
    inventory_velocity.py             # absorption, aging, months of supply, price dispersion
    marketing_efficiency.py           # cost per lead/contract, channel efficiency, lead-quality matrix
    sales_team_broker_performance.py  # agent/team/broker rankings, broker concentration risk
    collections_risk.py               # receivables aging, forward obligations, recovery effectiveness
    cancellations.py                  # cancellation rate/timing/reasons, revenue leakage
    construction_handover.py          # schedule/cost variance, delay exposure, handover readiness
    customer_experience.py            # case volume, SLA, sentiment, journey-stage breakdown
    data_quality.py                   # 12-point integrity battery -> a disclosed Data Quality Score
    executive_scoring.py              # materiality-weighted Overall Business Health
    correlations.py                   # cross-domain root causes, financial exposure, recommendations
```

Two pairs of the spec's originally-requested modules are deliberately
combined rather than duplicated: **broker performance** and **sales
team performance** share `sales_team_broker_performance.py` (near-
identical KPI shapes — conversion quality, discipline, cancellation
rate — for two "who sold it" cuts of the same data), and
**construction delivery** and **handover readiness** share
`construction_handover.py` (handover risk is the direct downstream
consequence of a construction delay, and both work at the same
building-level grain). Pricing/discount analytics live inside
`sales_performance.py` rather than a separate module, since discounting
is a sales-performance lever analyzed against the same project/phase
cuts sales performance already uses. `analysis/executive_scoring.py`
recovers the two separate health-score line items config expects
(`construction_delivery_health`, `handover_readiness_health`) from that
module's own component breakdown, so no score is lost to the merge.

## Executive Health Scores

Every domain module computes its own documented 0–100 health score
from a weighted blend of 3–4 named components (see each module's
`HEALTH_SCORE_WEIGHTS` and the `methodology` field it returns). Status
bands (Healthy / Watch / At Risk / Critical) and every weight live in
`config/real_estate_demo.yml`, not scattered through the code.

**Overall Business Health** is a materiality-weighted blend of all
nine domain scores — never a naive average. Collections (0.18),
construction delivery (0.16), cancellations (0.12), and customer
experience (0.12) carry more weight than marketing efficiency (0.05)
or inventory (0.06), because a receivables or delivery problem is
slower and more expensive to unwind than a soft marketing or
inventory-mix quarter.

## Root-Cause & Confidence Methodology

`analysis/correlations.py` cross-references the nine domains' own
outputs — never recomputing a number they already own — using only
descriptive statistics: Pearson correlation across project-level
aggregates, composite z-score summation for multi-metric root causes,
and coefficient of variation for seasonality detection. No machine
learning, no forecasting.

Every confidence score uses one transparent formula: a base score of
`0.35` for a real, measured pattern, plus up to `0.35` for the
strength of the underlying statistic, plus `0.10` per independent
signal that corroborates the same conclusion (up to 3), capped at
`0.97` — never full certainty. All four numbers are configurable in
`config/real_estate_demo.yml`.

## Report Structure

The rendered PDF (target ~12–18 A4 portrait pages) walks through: cover
& executive alert, executive dashboard (9 curated KPI cards), business
health overview, the nine domain sections in turn, cross-functional
root causes, financial exposure, a 90-day (phased through longer-term)
action plan, department accountability, forecast & outlook, "if no
action is taken," executive conclusion, and an appendix (methodology,
confidence methodology, Data Quality Score, and data limitations).
Only the report-wide narrative layer is AI-written; every domain
section's tables, rankings, and charts are rendered directly from
Python-computed data.

## Setup

**Requirements:** Python 3.12+, a Groq API key.

```bash
# 1. Clone the repository and move into it
cd solven-operations-intelligence

# 2. Create and activate a virtual environment
python3 -m venv .venv
source .venv/bin/activate   # Windows: .venv\Scripts\activate

# 3. Install dependencies
pip install -r requirements.txt
playwright install --with-deps chromium

# 4. Configure environment variables
cp .env.example .env
# then edit .env and add your Groq API key

# 5. (Re)generate the synthetic datasets (already committed in data/,
#    only needed if you want to regenerate them)
python generate_real_estate_data.py

# 6. Run the full pipeline
python app.py
```

## Generated Reports

Each run writes the same executive report in four formats to
`outputs/`, all built from one report dict (the Markdown, HTML, and
PDF renderers are pure, deterministic functions over that dict — no
analytics or AI reasoning happens during rendering):

- **`outputs/executive_report.json`** — the structured report: every
  score, ranking, root cause, confidence value, financial-exposure
  figure, and narrative field. The source of truth for the other three
  formats.
- **`outputs/executive_report.md`** — a plain-text Markdown rendering.
- **`outputs/executive_report.html`** — a self-contained, print-first
  HTML document (dark cover, light editorial body, no external fonts/
  scripts/CDN dependencies).
- **`outputs/executive_report.pdf`** — the primary client-facing
  artifact: an A4 portrait PDF rendered from the HTML via headless
  Chromium (Playwright), with real print margins and a repeating
  "Confidential · Page X of Y" footer.

```bash
open outputs/executive_report.pdf
```

## API (n8n integration)

`api.py` is a thin FastAPI wrapper around `app.run_pipeline()` — the
same orchestration function `python app.py` calls. It adds no
analytics of its own.

```bash
uvicorn api:app --reload --port 8000
```

- **`GET /health`** — liveness check.
- **`POST /run-analysis`** — runs the full pipeline synchronously
  (blocks until all four output files are written) and returns
  `report_id`, `generated_at`, the four `outputs` paths, and a summary
  (overall business health score, highest-risk project, highest-
  priority initiative). A failed run returns `500` with a clean
  message, never a raw stack trace. Only one run is allowed at a time;
  an overlapping call gets `409` immediately. n8n is expected to own
  scheduling this endpoint, downloading `outputs/executive_report.pdf`
  once it succeeds, emailing it, and handling retry/failure alerts —
  this endpoint has no authentication yet and is not intended to be
  deployed publicly as-is.

## GitHub Actions

`.github/workflows/run-analysis.yml` (`workflow_dispatch`) checks out
the repo, installs dependencies and Playwright's Chromium, runs
`python app.py` against the committed `data/*.csv` files, verifies all
four `outputs/executive_report.*` files were produced, and uploads
them as a build artifact. It requires a `GROQ_API_KEY` repository
secret.

## Tests

```bash
pytest tests/ -v
```

The suite (`tests/`) covers: deterministic data generation and
relational integrity, health-score and confidence bounds, funnel-stage
ordering, receivables-aging reconciliation, cancellation-rate
consistency, financial-exposure sign/structure, that all five
engineered root causes are discovered, full report-schema completeness
via a scripted mock AI provider (no network calls, no `GROQ_API_KEY`
required), and that no leftover logistics/warehouse terminology
appears anywhere in the rendered report output.

## Adapting This to a Real Client

1. Replace `data/*.csv` with the client's real exports (or build a
   connector into their CRM/ERP) matching the same 14-table schema —
   every analytics module validates its required columns up front and
   fails with a clear message if the shape doesn't match.
2. Edit `config/real_estate_demo.yml`: company name, currency, sales/
   collections/cancellation targets, health-score weights and status
   thresholds, and every anomaly threshold — nothing client-specific
   is hardcoded in `analysis/*.py`.
3. Re-tune `analysis/correlations.py`'s root-cause thresholds (e.g.
   `BROKER_DEPENDENCY_SHARE_MIN_PCT`, `SEASONALITY_CV_MIN`) if the
   client's portfolio is a meaningfully different shape (many more
   projects, a single-project developer, no broker channel, etc.).
4. Point `n8n` at the client's real delivery channel (email, Slack,
   a client portal) instead of a manual PDF download.

## Limitations

- All data in this repository is synthetic; the specific figures,
  findings, and dollar amounts in any generated report are
  demonstration output only.
- The AI narrative layer occasionally blends two different context
  figures into one sentence (e.g. citing a portfolio-wide exposure
  total inside a single project's headline) — every individual number
  it uses is still real and sourced from the given context, so this is
  an attribution-precision nuance, not a fabricated figure; the
  hallucination check would catch a genuinely invented number.
- `analysis/correlations.py`'s Pearson correlations are computed
  across only five projects — a statistically thin sample on its own,
  which is why every correlation finding is paired with a plain-
  language "is the same entity worst on both metrics" check rather
  than reported in isolation (see that module's own `methodology`
  output).
- No production authentication, rate limiting, or multi-tenant support
  exists in `api.py`.
